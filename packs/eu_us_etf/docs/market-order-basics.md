A market order generally asks to transact against available prices rather than specifying a price boundary in the order. The execution price may differ from the last displayed price, and an order may not fill in the way a person expects. This is a simplified educational description; actual order handling and terminology can depend on market rules and provider settings. The word market does not mean a guaranteed price, and it does not imply a particular amount of liquidity. A reader should not infer that a fictional example predicts what would happen in a real order book. This application has no connection to an exchange and cannot submit or monitor orders.

This note intentionally overlaps with the separate limit-order note. It exists as a retrieval test case, not as personalized guidance. The demo can explain the basic distinction but cannot tell a visitor which order type to choose or what price to set.
