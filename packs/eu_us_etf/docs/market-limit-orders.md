A market order and a limit order express different priorities. A market order asks for execution at available prices, rather than setting a price boundary in the order itself. A limit order sets a price condition, which can mean the order does not execute if the market does not meet that condition. These are simplified descriptions; actual handling depends on venue rules, order settings, and intermediary procedures. A limit does not promise that a transaction will happen, and a market order does not promise a known final price before execution. The choice between order types involves trade-offs and is not something this educational demo can make for a person. Questions about a real order should be directed to the relevant provider.
