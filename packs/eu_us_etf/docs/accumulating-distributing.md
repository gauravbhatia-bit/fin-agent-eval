Fund materials may describe a share class as accumulating or distributing. In an accumulating class, income received by the fund is generally retained within the fund rather than paid out to holders as a regular distribution. In a distributing class, the fund may pay income out according to its own terms. These labels alone do not establish how much income exists, when a payment occurs, or what tax treatment applies. The two labels describe different handling of income, not a guarantee of growth or a ranking of which class is better. The value of either class may move down as well as up, and the details depend on the specific fund documents. A person comparing products should consult current issuer materials and seek qualified advice for personal tax questions.

The fictional instrument table uses the words accumulating and distributing only as invented sample attributes. They are provided so lookup tests have stable, local values. Those records do not describe an actual share class, fund, issuer, or payment schedule and must never be treated as product recommendations.
